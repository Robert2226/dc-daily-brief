# DC Daily Brief — Friday, July 31, 2026
_Covers news through July 31, 2026._

## Equinix
- **Equinix's active expansion pipeline spans roughly 51,900 cabinets and $6.5 billion of planned investment** — The company's latest project schedule covers major IBX builds across the Americas, EMEA and Asia-Pacific, with delivery dates extending into 2028. The pipeline shows how corporate growth depends on dozens of local programs reaching power, construction, commissioning and customer-readiness gates on different schedules. [Equinix SEC Filing](https://investor.equinix.com/sec-filings/current-reports/content/0001101239-26-000032/0001101239-26-000032.pdf)
> **In Practice · Report capacity by readiness state, not one grand total.** Separate planned, funded, under construction, utility-ready, commissioned and customer-ready capacity, with confidence and constraint at each stage. A cabinet or megawatt should move stages only when its evidence gate is met. This prevents distant pipeline capacity from being mistaken for inventory operations can actually sell and support.

## Google
- **Google and Energy Dome launch a 23MW/200MWh long-duration storage project in Ireland** — The CO2 Battery installation at a former thermal power station will absorb surplus renewable energy and dispatch it during grid stress. Google describes the project as a blueprint for combining data-center growth with firm, affordable clean power rather than matching consumption only with annual renewable-energy purchases. [Google](https://blog.google/intl/en-ie/company-news/google-and-energy-dome-launch-energy-storage-project/)
> **In Practice · Define the operating value before celebrating the technology.** Document the storage system's charge window, discharge duration, response time, availability target, maintenance mode and grid dispatch authority. Then map which facility risks it actually reduces and which still require UPS and generator coverage. A program succeeds when the new asset has a precise role in the resilience stack, not merely an innovative label.

## Competitors
- **CyrusOne begins a three-building, $1.5 billion campus beside a Texas gas plant** — The 760MW Fairfield project will comprise three roughly 306,000-square-foot buildings supplied by Calpine's 1GW Freestone combined-cycle facility; two buildings are underway and the third is due to start in August, with completion targeted for August 2027. No tenants were identified in the filings, separating construction momentum from contracted occupancy. [Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/cyrusone-files-to-develop-three-buildings-for-15bn-data-center-campus-in-fairfield-texas/)
> **In Practice · Co-location with generation does not remove interface risk.** Build a responsibility matrix for plant output, transmission path, substation, metering, protection settings, black start, maintenance coordination and fuel interruption. Test the sequence for loss and restoration of the dedicated source. Physical proximity shortens the electrical path; disciplined ownership makes that path dependable.

## DC Infrastructure
- **CenterPoint adds $1.2 billion to its capital plan as more than 17GW of large loads enter ERCOT's Batch Zero process** — About 14GW may qualify for system-wide study, and the utility still expects to energize up to 8GW of Greater Houston data-center load by 2029. Studying large requests together should expose their combined grid effect, but it also means one project's outcome depends on assumptions and changes across the whole batch. [Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/centerpoint-energy-raises-investment-plan-by-12bn-as-data-center-load-pipeline-grows/)
> **In Practice · Manage the queue as an external dependency with scenarios.** Record the study case, competing-load assumptions, required network upgrades, decision dates and who controls each input. Maintain best, expected and constrained energization scenarios instead of one promised date. When a shared study changes, update commercial, construction and commissioning plans together so one stale schedule does not become the unofficial truth.

## AI & Compute Demand
- **CleanCore signs a 10-year colocation agreement built around Cerebras systems** — The agreement gives Cerebras a long-duration infrastructure commitment while the AI-chip company also expands into CrowdStrike's security platform. A decade-long term can support capital investment, but it makes refresh cycles, power-density changes, service levels and exit conditions more consequential than in a short capacity lease. [Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/cleancore-solutions-signs-on-cerebras-under-10-year-colocation-agreement/)
> **In Practice · Design long contracts for technology that will not stand still.** Define power and cooling envelopes, refresh rights, migration windows, stranded-capacity treatment, spares, performance acceptance and end-of-life responsibilities. Schedule architecture reviews before each major hardware generation. The goal is a stable commercial relationship with controlled technical change—not freezing today's design for ten years.

## New AI Models & Releases
- **Meta releases Muse Image and previews Muse Video for generation and editing across its apps** — Muse Image supports instruction-based editing and multi-reference composition and is rolling into Meta AI, Instagram Stories and limited WhatsApp markets; Muse Video is an early preview. The launch expands model evaluation beyond text into provenance, consent, brand controls and review of media that can look production-ready before it is operationally safe. [Meta AI](https://ai.meta.com/blog/introducing-muse-image-muse-video-msl/)
> **In Practice · Add media-specific acceptance criteria.** Test identity preservation, unwanted object insertion, text accuracy, copyright and consent risks, watermarking, metadata retention and repeatability across representative prompts. Require human approval for external use and retain the source references and final decision. Visual quality is only one dimension of a supportable production workflow.

## Networking
- **Deutsche Telekom contributes StratoWeave to LF Networking for open, model-driven transport automation** — The candidate project uses declarative orchestration to configure and manage complex networks and aims to reduce dependence on proprietary automation stacks. Open automation can improve portability and collaboration, but the operational risk moves into data models, controller permissions and how safely desired state becomes live change. [Linux Foundation](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/lf-networking-expands-portfolio-with-new-stratoweave-project-contributed-by-deutsche-telekom-to-advance-open-ai-native-transport-network-automation)
> **In Practice · Treat network intent as controlled code.** Put models and configurations through peer review, validation, staged rollout and automated pre/post checks; define blast-radius limits and a tested rollback. Separate the authority to propose a change from the authority to deploy it. Zero-touch should mean repeatable controls with fewer manual steps—not fewer accountable humans.

## Backend / Cloud & Data
- **Enterprise cloud-infrastructure spending reaches $143 billion in Q2** — Synergy Research reports an eleventh consecutive quarter of accelerating growth, driven heavily by AI workloads. The market signal is strong, but for an individual program rising cloud consumption can reflect delivered value, inefficient architecture or both unless usage is linked to owners and outcomes. [Data Center Dynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/enterprise-cloud-infrastructure-spending-hits-143-billion-report/)
> **In Practice · Pair every material cloud cost with an owner and a unit of value.** Tag workloads by product, environment and team, then track cost per transaction, model run, customer or other useful output alongside reliability. Review idle resources and anomalous growth on a fixed cadence. FinOps becomes program management when financial, technical and business evidence drive one prioritization conversation.

## Program & PM
- **PMI projects demand for project professionals to grow faster than the available talent supply** — PMI's global talent-gap research estimates the profession could grow from about 39.6 million people in 2025 while organizations face an expanding shortfall through the next decade. For technical operators, the opportunity belongs to people who can translate field reality into scope, risk, sequencing and decisions—not those who abandon their operational expertise. [PMI Talent Gap](https://www.pmi.org/-/media/pmi/documents/public/pdf/learning/thought-leadership/talent_gap_2025-v3.pdf)
> **In Practice · Build a portfolio of evidence before seeking the title.** For one current initiative, keep the charter, stakeholder map, schedule, risk log, decision record, change history and lessons learned; quantify the operational outcome. Ask to own coordination across one additional team or vendor. PMP study builds the vocabulary, while documented delivery proves you can apply it under real constraints.
