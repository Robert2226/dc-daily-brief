01Equinix

Equinix describes a shared-services architecture on Amazon EKS

September 17 case study, coauthored by AWS and Equinix contributors. The design separates workload, platform and networking accounts, with shared platform services and managed Kubernetes. The authors report 40% lower operational overhead and four times the deployment frequency after the change. These are reported outcomes for the described implementation, not a benchmark for every Equinix environment. The useful architectural question is which dependencies become common as teams consolidate services.

AWS architecture case study

In Practice · Draw the recovery dependency too

A shared service can simplify ordinary delivery while becoming essential to restoration. Record whether teams can obtain configuration, credentials and deployment artifacts when that service or its network path is unavailable. The recovery design deserves the same attention as the normal architecture.

02Google

Storage Intelligence Advisor reaches general availability

Listed in Google's September 14–18 Cloud update, updated September 18. The Cloud Storage capability surfaces issues and opportunities from storage metrics, including operational anomalies, errors and cross-region egress. Its documentation describes drilling into recommendations to understand the affected resources. This is a new analysis capability, not evidence that any particular customer's storage configuration is wrong.

Google's weekly release update · Advisor documentation

In Practice · Investigate the recommendation's boundary

Unexpected egress might be waste, an intended replication flow or a recovery exercise. Identify the workload, destination and time interval before acting. A cost recommendation should be tested against resilience and service requirements as well as the bill.

03Incidents, Reliability & Remediation

AWS updates the recovery limits after Middle East facility damage

Incident began in March; material public assessment September 15, independently reported September 16. AWS says it cannot restore access to resources and data stored exclusively in UAE zone mec1-az2; work continues on regional resources and the other zones. For Bahrain, AWS says damage across multiple zones exceeded its regional resilience design and it cannot recover resources held exclusively in that region. AWS previously confirmed drone-related physical damage, power disruption and fire-suppression water damage. Customers must use available copies elsewhere or alternatives. The remaining UAE recovery scope and timing are unresolved; the next Bahrain update, expected early 2027, is not a promised restoration date.

AWS public incident updates · Independent reporting and UAE clarification

Computle publishes a preliminary UKB power-outage report

September 15 incident: 36 users across five customers lost workstations after UPS B02 maintenance isolation; service was restored that evening. Computle's working theory is that both feeds became unavailable, but the facility provider's account is still pending: that is not a confirmed root cause. Its response includes on-site investigation and proposed resilience improvements, alongside a planned move to UKC by Christmas. The report does not yet demonstrate that those measures prevent recurrence.

Computle's preliminary report

In Practice · Separate restoration from prevention

Restoring workloads, identifying the failure mechanism and proving a corrective measure are three different milestones. Track the evidence for each. Multiple feeds or zones protect only against failures that remain inside their actual independence boundary.

04DC Infrastructure

Crusoe raises $3.9 billion for its AI infrastructure build-out

September 18 reporting. DCD reports a Series F led by Atreides, Mubadala and Valor, valuing Crusoe at $30.9 billion, with NVIDIA among the participants. Crusoe says it has more than 1 GW operational and 6 GW contracted; those are company-reported figures with different delivery states. The financing strengthens its ability to pursue the pipeline, while power, equipment and commissioning remain separate execution requirements.

DCD financing report

In Practice · Preserve the capacity states

Funded, contracted, energized and accepted by a customer describe different achievements. A delivery view should retain these distinctions for each phase. Adding all announced MW into an operating-capacity total conceals the work and dependencies still ahead.

05DCOS, DCIM, BMS & Controls

EnvisionSCADA's September release adds Git configuration workflows and migration work

September 14 release notes, inspected September 19. The new version adds configuration export and Git commit/push/pull workflows while replacing DataTable with DataGrid. Some queries need manual attention during migration. The notes also say database connection strings enter project exports and Git configuration, including a password when supplied inline. Review credential handling before adopting the workflow; this documented behavior is not a newly alleged exploit. No independent deployment evidence was found for this release.

Vendor release and migration notes

In Practice · Version history is one part of recoverability

A SCADA configuration commit does not prove that local controls, database schemas and credentials can be restored together. Test a representative migration in an isolated environment, inspect exceptions and verify the approved rollback path. Supervisory software and local protective control retain different responsibilities.

06AI & Compute Demand

Bitdeer AI commits to 65.1 MW in Johor

September 18 reporting. DCD describes a ten-year lease for the A202 facility, expected to energize in the third quarter of 2027. The planned deployment supports liquid-cooled NVIDIA GB300 NVL72 systems. Together with the adjacent 21.7 MW A201 commitment, the campus allocation is 86.8 MW. A lease is concrete demand evidence, but the planned energization date and eventual workload utilization remain future outcomes.

DCD lease report

In Practice · Connect the contract to usable compute

An electrical allocation must still become racks, cooling capacity, network paths and accepted systems. Maintain a dependency chain from facility availability through accelerator delivery to workload readiness. A commercial commencement date alone cannot establish the first useful training or inference result.

07New AI Models & Releases

Anthropic releases optimized biomolecular-modeling code

September 17 research release. Anthropic describes using Claude to optimize more than 30 models in under four weeks. It reports roughly fourfold speedups with small precision tradeoffs and nearly twofold improvements while preserving identical outputs in the relevant comparisons. The release concerns optimized scientific-model implementations; it is not a new general-purpose Claude model or proof of scientific validity. The useful development is a public performance-engineering case whose numerical and hardware conditions can be inspected.

Anthropic research and code links

In Practice · Preserve the acceptance criterion

A faster implementation may alter numerical behavior. Compare the same input, hardware and output-quality requirements, then record whether equivalence is exact or tolerance-based. Performance and correctness need separate evidence even when an AI tool generated the optimization.

08Networking

EXA announces its ninth transatlantic cable, Meridian

September 16 announcement, carried September 17. The planned 6,552 km route links New Jersey with Brean in the UK, with service targeted for 2029. EXA describes 24 fiber pairs and more than 500 Tb/s of design capacity, alongside onward routes toward Slough. The construction contract milestone advances the project; its design capacity is not yet available bandwidth.

EXA announcement via Telecom Ramblings

In Practice · Follow diversity beyond the beach

Two ocean routes can still converge at a landing station, duct, power source or metropolitan building. Compare the whole service path, including terrestrial tails and restoration arrangements. Separate physical route diversity from the commercial availability of capacity on that route.

09Backend / Cloud & Data

Azure PostgreSQL's September maintenance adds a connection metric

Release notes updated September 15; changes apply to new servers from September 11 and existing servers through scheduled maintenance, with worldwide rollout taking up to a month. The release introduces the preview PgBouncer client_connections_total metric and platform hardening, without a PostgreSQL minor-version update. A published release note therefore does not mean every existing server already has the change.

Microsoft maintenance notes

In Practice · Interpret connections before changing the pool

Total client connections can include active, idle, waiting and authenticating clients. It is not a query-throughput measure. Read it beside queueing, database saturation and response time before treating a larger connection limit as additional service capacity.

10Program & PM

DCD's workforce survey finds expansion outpacing staffing

September 18 reporting. More than two-thirds of surveyed developers and operators reported staffing below requirements; nearly a third reported staffing below 80% of demand. DCD highlights gaps across facilities, power, cooling and IT operations. These are survey findings, not a census or a staffing benchmark for a particular site. For delivery leaders, the immediate question is which operational and improvement commitments compete for the same scarce people.

DCD Intelligence survey report

In Practice · Resource the evidence of improvement

Corrective work includes design, implementation and a meaningful effectiveness check. If the plan funds only implementation, actions can be marked complete while the original risk remains untested. Give the verification work an owner and protected capacity.
