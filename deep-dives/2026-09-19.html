01Physical · Connect fuel handling, controls and recovery

Deep dive

Synthesis: follow the fuel, the authority and the recovery

Connect the lessons at one boundary

The eighth expanded edition brings earlier lessons about capacity, local controls, shared dependencies, thermal time and electrical load steps into one hypothetical Hall A scenario. This is an engineering teaching example, not a reconstruction of an operator's incident or an Equinix procedure. Imagine a bulk tank supplying a day tank through a transfer system, with the day tank feeding standby generation. The system's useful outcome is dependable fuel delivery without an uncontrolled release. Tank volume alone cannot establish that outcome.

Start by drawing the physical flow, including valves, pumps, return paths and containment. Then draw the authority over that flow: which instrument measures level, which logic requests transfer, and which final element can actually stop it. A screen displaying “pump stopped” is evidence about a reported state, not necessarily proof that liquid flow has ceased. Gravity, siphoning or another supply path may matter in a particular design. Qualified engineering must establish the actual mechanism.

Turn remaining space into a time boundary

Suppose the hypothetical tank has 300 litres of usable space before an unacceptable level and receives 60 litres per minute. Ignoring simultaneous consumption, it has five minutes at that rate. This is an illustrative balance, not a design allowance: detection delay, sensor uncertainty, stopping time and flow variation reduce the usable response margin. A ten-minute supervisory polling interval plainly cannot provide the assumed response by itself.

An independent high-level protective function might stop transfer before that boundary. Independence needs evidence about the sensor, logic, power and final element; two labels on the same supervisory screen do not establish it. EPA's technical alert documents failures of overfill-prevention systems, making maintenance and verification of the protective function a concrete reliability concern rather than an assumption based on its presence.

EPA technical alert

Separate supervision from local protection

A supervisory system can trend levels, coordinate operations and alert people. Local control and protective functions need behavior appropriate to their timing and consequences, including when communications are unavailable. NIST's operational-technology guidance emphasizes that these systems interact with physical processes and have distinct reliability and safety requirements. That distinction helps frame the review; it does not prescribe this hypothetical tank's wiring or setpoints.

NIST SP 800-82 Revision 3

Now add a common dependency: the normal level signal and the protective signal share a power supply. Loss of that supply might remove both observations. The right question is what the installed equipment does in that state, supported by design and test evidence. A diverse communications path would not, by itself, fix a shared sensing-power problem. The failure boundary must follow the physical dependencies rather than the drawing's organizational boxes.

Recovery changes the available capacity

In the scenario, isolating a suspect transfer path may prevent a release while reducing fuel availability to generation. Containment and service continuity are related decisions with different success criteria. Reopening a path simply because the dashboard looks normal would leave the initiating condition unresolved. Conversely, leaving a system isolated indefinitely without revisiting the service plan could exhaust another reserve.

After qualified personnel establish safe conditions and the approved recovery path, electrical restoration still has dynamics. Earlier lessons showed why continuous generator MW do not prove acceptance of a sudden load step. Restarting several groups and recharging batteries can create a different demand profile from the pre-event state. Cummins describes transient performance through departures from nominal voltage and frequency and subsequent recovery. Apply that distinction when defining representative commissioning evidence.

Cummins transient-performance guidance

Ask for a connected demonstration

For the hypothetical review, connect four pieces of evidence: the material balance and response time, the protective function under relevant failures, the remaining service capacity during isolation, and the staged return to load. Include equipment unavailable for maintenance and loss of supervisory communications in the agreed test scope. Preserve the event record so apparent recovery does not erase the evidence needed to understand the failure.

The synthesis is a judgment exercise: a tank, alarm, redundant source and restart plan can each look satisfactory separately while the connections remain weak. The PgPM can expose the missing interface evidence and bring the responsible disciplines together. That contribution is more useful than treating four completed component checklists as proof that the whole sequence works.

Why can a fuel-system isolation improve safety while reducing service resilience?

It can stop a hazardous transfer path while also removing fuel supply needed by generation. The remaining reserve, permitted operating state and recovery sequence must be assessed together. A normal supervisory indication alone does not prove the path is safe to restore.

02Logical · Keep recovery independent of the failure

Deep dive

Make recovery work when its usual management path fails

Distinguish running from changing

A service may continue handling requests even when the systems used to configure or repair it are unavailable. The data plane does the ordinary work; the control plane changes its configuration or capacity. AWS's static-stability guidance advocates preparing enough working capacity before failure so that surviving infrastructure does not immediately depend on new provisioning. This is a useful design principle, not a guarantee that every service continues indefinitely without management.

AWS Builders' Library

Today's Equinix architecture case illustrates why this distinction matters as organizations share platform services. Consolidation can improve normal delivery, while recovery still depends on the reachability of artifacts, identity systems and network configuration. Those dependencies need explicit failure cases. Having a documented command is insufficient when its credentials or endpoint disappear with the system it is meant to repair.

Learn from a dated, bounded report

Azure's public review of its July 23 West US connectivity incident provides a background case, not a new September outage. Microsoft reports that a change intended for one optical device expanded across data-center egress devices. Device-by-device checks missed the aggregate effect, and an automated rollback depended on connectivity disrupted by the incident. Manual recovery restored connectivity. The report lists completed scope and validation fixes alongside further corrective work with target dates; a target date is not proof of completion.

Azure incident history and July 23 review

The engineering interpretation is that the recovery mechanism belonged partly to the same failure boundary. That interpretation follows the published account; it does not establish a hidden root cause or prove that the same architecture exists elsewhere. It gives a precise question to ask of a different system: what must still be reachable for this recovery action to succeed?

Work through the surviving capacity

Imagine a hypothetical application serving 900 equal-cost requests per second across three zones. Each zone has already provisioned, tested capacity for 600 per second. If one zone is lost, the two survivors have 1,200 per second in this simplified model. They can accommodate the workload without launching new instances, provided routing and the remaining dependencies work as assumed.

If two zones are lost, only 600 per second remains. The same design no longer carries the whole load. Admission control, previously studied, may preserve selected work; it does not conjure the missing capacity. Real capacity also depends on the request mix and contention, so the arithmetic is a starting hypothesis for representative tests. It is not a universal sizing rule or a substitute for regional recovery.

Find the circular dependency

Suppose Hall A's recovery procedure says to retrieve an emergency configuration from a vault, then restore a network route. The vault is reachable only through that route. The procedure has a loop even if every command is correct. A similar loop appears when starting recovery machines requires the identity service being restored, or when the only runbook sits behind the unavailable authentication system.

An approved design could provide independently reachable recovery information, appropriately protected credentials and a tested management path. The appropriate controls depend on the system and the consequences of access. The point is to demonstrate independence without introducing uncontrolled privileges. Backups also need a readable copy, a functioning restore process and sufficient destination capacity; possession of a backup file alone cannot establish recovery time.

Test the missing dependency deliberately

A useful rehearsal removes the normal management path in a controlled environment and observes whether the approved recovery sequence still works. Record elapsed time, decision authority, available evidence and dependencies discovered during execution. Test the return to service as well as the first successful administrative login. Customer-facing probes can reveal that equipment is reachable while the actual service remains broken.

This advances the earlier queue and reconciliation lessons: deciding what work to accept and retry is still necessary, but now the recovery process itself is under test. The acceptance question is whether the service can reach its agreed operating state under the stated failure, using capabilities that demonstrably survive it. A green device status or a successful exercise with all management services available answers a narrower question.

What is wrong with a rollback that must cross the network path being repaired?

It may become unavailable precisely when needed. Demonstrate an approved recovery path whose essential information, credentials and connectivity survive the specified failure, then test it with the normal path absent.

03PgPM Growth · Turn incident learning into verified improvement

Lead the learning review around evidence and decisions

A useful incident review explains how people and systems arrived at the outcome with the information available at the time. Google's SRE guidance treats blameless postmortems as a way to expose contributing conditions and improve systems. For a cross-functional PgPM, the practical skill is facilitating a shared timeline without collapsing uncertainty into consensus. Separate observations, participant accounts and hypotheses. Ask what a person could see, which choices were available, and which interface made a reasonable action fragile. Blameless does not mean ownerless: decisions, evidence gaps and follow-up work still need accountable owners.

Google SRE postmortem culture

Close the risk on effectiveness evidence

Implementing an action and demonstrating its effect are different achievements. GAO's review of nuclear-safety recommendations illustrates the importance of evaluating whether corrective actions resolve the underlying concern. Translate that principle into an explicit closure claim: which failure condition should now be prevented, detected or contained, and what evidence would contradict that claim? Set the verification method before implementation is declared complete. A receipt for training, an installed component or an approved document can prove an activity happened without proving the original problem is controlled.

GAO on evaluating corrective-action effectiveness

Worked example · A review that changes the closure decision

Imagine a hypothetical Hall A test in which a transfer alarm reaches the supervisory screen late. No release occurs, and the team initially proposes “retrain operators.” This is an invented leadership example; it is not a finding about a real incident or an Equinix system.

In the review, the PgPM asks for synchronized controller and supervisory timestamps, the expected alarm path, and the operating assumptions used in the test. The group records that the display was late but has not yet established where the delay originated. “The operator missed it” remains an unsupported causal claim. An instrumentation owner investigates detection timing, a controls owner checks propagation, and operations explains which indications were available. The meeting ends with explicit evidence gaps rather than a premature unanimous cause.

Suppose subsequent controlled investigation establishes a configuration delay in the supervisory path. The proposed action becomes changing that configuration and verifying alarm delivery under representative traffic and a failed communications path. Engineering separately confirms which local protection must work without the supervisor. This avoids assuming that faster notification can substitute for an independently required protective function.

The closure record now has two stages. Implementation evidence identifies the reviewed configuration and its deployment. Effectiveness evidence demonstrates the agreed response envelope in the specified cases, with exceptions recorded and accepted by the responsible authority. If the failed-path case remains untested, the implementation can be complete while the risk action stays open. The PgPM makes that distinction visible and resources the missing verification rather than turning it into an administrative overdue item.
